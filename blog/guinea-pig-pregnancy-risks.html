Is Your Guinea Pig Pregnant? What You Should Know

November 16, 2026

It usually starts with a surprise. You brought home two "sisters" from a pet store or a friend's litter, and a few weeks later one of them is getting round in a way that doesn't look like too many pellets. Or a new guinea pig joined the herd and nobody double-checked who was a boy.

If you think your guinea pig might be pregnant, you haven't done anything wrong. Guinea pigs are hard to sex when they are young, and mistakes are very common. What matters now is knowing what to watch for and getting her checked.

Why guinea pig pregnancy is riskier than you'd think

Guinea pigs are built differently from many other small pets. Their babies, called pups, are born furred, with their eyes open, and ready to run around within hours. That's lovely to see. It also means each pup is large compared with its mother, and a big pup has to fit through a small pelvis.

The pelvis itself is part of the problem. During birth, the joint at the bottom of the pelvis needs to widen to let pups through. In some females, especially those having their first litter after they are fully grown, that joint doesn't widen enough. A pup can get stuck, and that is an emergency.

Even when the pelvis is fine, things can go wrong. Sometimes the uterus simply doesn't contract and labor never starts, even though the pups are alive. A recent veterinary case report described exactly that in a young first-time mother: she needed a cesarean section, both pups survived, and sadly she did not. I mention it because it shows something families should hear early. Surgery for a guinea pig in labor is serious, and the mother is the one most at risk.

Pregnant guinea pigs can also develop a dangerous metabolic problem late in pregnancy, often called pregnancy toxemia. It's more likely if she stops eating, is overweight, or is stressed.

Signs she may be pregnant

  • A rounder belly, especially low and toward the back
  • Eating and drinking more than usual
  • Weight gain on your kitchen scale
  • Sometimes you can see or feel movement along her sides late in pregnancy

These signs can also come from other problems, including ovarian cysts, which are very common in female guinea pigs. An exam, and often an ultrasound, tells us what is really going on.

What to do if you think she's pregnant

Separate the male right away. A female can become pregnant again very soon after giving birth, so even if you are too late to prevent this pregnancy, you can prevent the next one.

Book an exam. We can confirm the pregnancy, estimate how far along she is, and check her overall health. An ultrasound is often the best way to see the pups and check that they are alive.

Keep her eating. Unlimited hay, fresh greens, and a good guinea pig pellet with vitamin C matter even more now. A pregnant guinea pig who stops eating needs to be seen the same day.

Keep life calm. Avoid big changes in her housing or herd. Handle her gently and support her whole body.

Weigh her a few times a week. Write it down. Sudden weight loss in a pregnant guinea pig is a warning sign.

Should I breed my guinea pig?

Please talk with us before you decide. Everything above applies to a planned litter just as much as a surprise one, and the mother carries most of the risk. If you have a male and female who are bonded, neutering the male lets them live together without more litters. Spaying a female also prevents ovarian cysts and other reproductive problems later in life, which I cover in my post on ovarian cysts and spaying guinea pigs.

When to call your vet

Call right away if your pregnant guinea pig:

  • Is straining or pushing for more than a short time without a pup appearing
  • Has bloody, green, or foul-smelling discharge
  • Stops eating, seems weak, or is sitting hunched and quiet
  • Is drooling, wobbly, or breathing hard
  • Delivered some pups but seems to still be straining or unwell afterward
  • Seems overdue, with a big belly and no signs of labor

Book a visit soon if:

  • You think she might be pregnant
  • You aren't sure whether your guinea pigs are male or female
  • You have a male and female living together and want to talk about neutering or spaying
