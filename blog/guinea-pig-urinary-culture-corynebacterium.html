Guinea Pig Urinary Infections: Why Culture Is Worth Considering

October 2026

A guinea pig with hematuria or stranguria is one of the most common presentations we see. The usual pattern is a urinalysis, maybe radiographs to check for stones, and an empiric antibiotic. A new retrospective study in JAVMA suggests that empiric choice may be wrong more often than we'd like. Here is a summary from Dr. Logan Kopp, Nashville's guinea pig specialist, with some options to consider.

Some context first

Urinary disease is common in guinea pigs. In a separate retrospective study of 600 guinea pigs from a Budapest exotic-animal clinic published this quarter, urinary tract disease was the second most common problem (18.67%), after molar malocclusion (27.5%) (Keszeli et al.).

The study

Martinson and Mans reviewed 112 bacterial isolates from 63 clinically ill pet guinea pigs cultured between 2004 and 2023.

Key findings

  • Most common genera overall: Streptococcus (19%), Corynebacterium (17%) and Prevotella (11%).
  • Most common species: Corynebacterium renale (11%).
  • Urine was mostly Corynebacterium. It made up 53% of urinary isolates.
  • Corynebacterium susceptibility: doxycycline 94%, azithromycin 82%, enrofloxacin 71%, TMS 65%.
  • Across all urinary isolates, only 50% were TMS-susceptible and 59% enrofloxacin-susceptible.

What this means in practice

TMS and enrofloxacin are the go-to empiric antibiotics for many of us, partly because they're familiar and well tolerated. These data suggest they may miss a meaningful share of urinary infections in guinea pigs. It's a single-center retrospective culture dataset, so local patterns may differ, but it gives us a few options to think about:

  • Consider culture and susceptibility early, ideally from a cystocentesis sample, especially for recurrent cases, sows with repeated hematuria, or patients that haven't responded to a first antibiotic.
  • Think about Corynebacterium when choosing empirically. If you need to start treatment before culture results are back, these susceptibility patterns are worth weighing. Doxycycline and azithromycin covered Corynebacterium well in this dataset.
  • Re-culture when cases relapse, rather than switching blindly between drug classes.
  • Look for the reason behind it. Uroliths, ovarian disease in intact sows, pain and reduced water intake can all sit behind a "UTI." Imaging and a full exam are still part of the workup.
  • Remember the gut. Any antibiotic choice in a hindgut fermenter needs the usual attention to appetite, fecal output and supportive care.
