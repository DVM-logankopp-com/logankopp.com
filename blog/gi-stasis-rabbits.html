Understanding GI Stasis in Rabbits

October 2026

Gastrointestinal stasis is one of the most common reasons rabbits are presented for emergency care. It is not a single disease but a syndrome in which the normal movement of the gut slows or stops. Because rabbits are obligate herbivores with a specialized digestive system, any slowdown can become serious quickly.

Owners often notice reduced or absent fecal pellets, decreased appetite, lethargy, or a hunched posture. These signs can develop over hours and should be treated as urgent. Early veterinary evaluation is important because underlying causes—pain, dental disease, dehydration, diet changes, or other illness—need to be identified and addressed.

At Priest Lake Veterinary Hospital, evaluation of a rabbit with suspected GI stasis typically includes a physical examination, assessment of hydration, and imaging when indicated. Supportive care may involve fluid therapy, pain management, assisted feeding, and treatment of any primary problem. Many rabbits recover well when care is started promptly.

Prevention focuses on consistent high-fiber diet (unlimited grass hay), appropriate fresh greens, limited pellets and treats, and regular veterinary checkups that include dental evaluation. Any change in appetite or fecal output warrants a call to the clinic.
