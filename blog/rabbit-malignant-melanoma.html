Malignant Melanoma in Rabbits: What an 11-Case Series Adds

October 2026

Skin and subcutaneous masses in older rabbits are something we encounter in practice all the time. Most of the time we're thinking abscess, a benign skin tumor, or something we can excise and forget about. A new retrospective series in the Journal of Exotic Pet Medicine is a good reminder to keep malignant melanoma on the list, especially around the head and eyes. Here is a summary from Dr. Logan Kopp, Nashville's rabbit specialist, with some options to consider for your own cases.

The study

Ishitobi and colleagues reviewed 11 rabbits with spontaneous malignant melanoma seen between 2007 and 2024. Melanoma is not something most of us diagnose often in rabbits, so even a small series helps us know what it looks like.

Key findings

  • Older rabbits. The mean age was 7.6 years.
  • More males. Seven of the 11 rabbits were male.
  • Head first. The 11 rabbits had 13 tumors, and the head was the most common site (8 of 13).
  • Periocular masses stood out. Five of 13 tumors were periocular.
  • Scrotal tumors occurred. Two of 13 were on the scrotum.
  • Treatment was mainly surgical excision.
  • Metastasis happened. Metastases were reported to the lungs and lymph nodes.
  • Prognosis was poor overall.

What this means in practice

This is a small retrospective series, so it can't tell us how common melanoma is or which treatment works best. It does give us a pattern to recognize. Some options to think about:

  • Sample periocular and facial masses in senior rabbits. A pigmented or unusual mass near the eye in a middle-aged or older rabbit may be worth a fine-needle aspirate or biopsy before it's simply removed, so the surgical plan and the owner conversation can match what it is.
  • Check the scrotum in intact males. Two tumors in this series were scrotal. Scrotal skin is easy to skip on a busy exam.
  • Consider staging before surgery. Lung and lymph node metastases were reported, so thoracic imaging and palpating (and if indicated, aspirating) regional lymph nodes can help guide the plan. Thoracic CT is one option when it's available.
  • Submit excised masses for histopathology. Knowing it's a melanoma changes the follow-up conversation.
  • Be honest about prognosis. In this series, outcomes were generally poor even with surgery. Owners can make better decisions when they hear that early, along with the reasons surgery may still be worthwhile for comfort or local control.
  • Plan rechecks. Given the metastatic pattern, periodic thoracic imaging and lymph node checks are reasonable things to discuss.

Facial and periocular surgery in rabbits can be demanding, with limited skin to work with around the eye and a patient that needs good analgesia and quick return to eating. These are cases worth planning carefully.

Citation

Ishitobi K, et al. Spontaneous malignant melanoma in rabbits: a report of 11 cases (2007–2024). J Exot Pet Med. 2026;59:22-27. doi:10.1053/j.jepm.2026.08.002
