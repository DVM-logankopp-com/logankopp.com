How to Choose an Exotic Vet in Nashville for Your Small Mammal

October 2026

If you share your home with a rabbit, guinea pig, ferret, chinchilla, hedgehog, sugar glider or pet rat, you already know they aren't small dogs or cats. Their teeth, digestion, anesthesia needs and ways of showing illness are all different. Finding the right exotic vet in Nashville before something goes wrong is one of the kindest things you can do for them.

Why species experience matters

Many exotic companion mammals are prey animals. In the wild, looking weak attracts predators, so they are very good at hiding pain and illness. By the time a rabbit stops eating or a guinea pig starts losing weight, the problem may have been building for a while.

A veterinarian who sees these species every day knows what "normal" looks like for each one. They know which small changes matter, which medications are safe, and how to handle a frightened animal gently. That experience makes a real difference in diagnosis, surgery and recovery.

Questions to ask when choosing a vet

  • Which species do you see regularly? Ask how often the clinic treats your type of pet, not just whether it can.
  • Who will examine my pet? At some hospitals only certain doctors see exotic patients, so it helps to book with them directly.
  • What diagnostics are available? Look for in-house bloodwork, digital X-rays and dental imaging, with access to advanced imaging such as CT when a case needs it.
  • How is anesthesia handled? Ask about monitoring, warming, pain control and how they help small mammals get back to eating after a procedure.
  • Is there a quiet space for prey animals? A calmer waiting area away from barking dogs reduces stress.
  • What happens after hours? Know the emergency plan before you need it.

What "board-certified" means

A board-certified veterinary specialist has completed additional training and passed a rigorous examination in one area of medicine. The American Board of Veterinary Practitioners (ABVP) offers a specialty in Exotic Companion Mammal Practice, covering rabbits, ferrets, rodents and other small mammals.

Rabbit-friendly care

Rabbit owners may want to look for a rabbit-friendly practice. Priest Lake Veterinary Hospital is listed as a Gold-level practice by the Rabbit Welfare Association and Fund (RWAF), and Dr. Kopp is the only Gold-level rabbit-friendly veterinarian certified by the RWAF in North America. Gold criteria include a separate rabbit waiting area, prey-species housing away from dogs and cats, and staff trained in rabbit care.

Working with your regular vet

You don't have to give up a primary veterinarian you trust. Many pets are referred to Dr. Kopp for a second opinion, dental disease, gastrointestinal or urinary problems, pain management, or surgery, and then return to their regular clinic for routine care.

When to call your vet

Don't wait for a routine appointment if your small mammal:

  • Has stopped eating or is eating much less
  • Has fewer, smaller or no droppings
  • Seems hunched, quiet or reluctant to move
  • Is breathing hard or with an open mouth
  • Is weak, wobbly, or has collapsed

For these species, "wait and see" can be risky.
