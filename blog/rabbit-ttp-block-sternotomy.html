Ultrasound-Guided TTP Block for Rabbit Sternotomy: Two Cases

October 2026

Median sternotomy is uncommon in pet rabbits, and when it happens the analgesic plan has to be solid. Local anesthetic blocks that we use in dogs and cats are still sparsely described in rabbits. Melega and colleagues report bilateral ultrasound-guided transversus thoracic plane (TTP) blocks as part of multimodal analgesia in two rabbits undergoing sternotomy and mediastinal mass excision. It is a small series, but it is practical.

The cases

Two client-household Lionhead rabbits underwent median sternotomy for mediastinal mass excision.

Local block: bilateral ultrasound-guided TTP blocks at the 2nd and 5th intercostal spaces. Bupivacaine 0.5% was used at a total dose of 2 mg/kg, split over four injection sites, with volumes of about 0.9 to 1.0 mL.

Systemic plan: premedication with methadone, dexmedetomidine, and midazolam; maintenance with isoflurane plus a ketamine CRI.

Intraoperatively, only one rabbit showed a brief nociceptive response. The second rabbit died about four hours after surgery from respiratory compromise related to thoracic drain malfunction. That death is a monitoring and equipment lesson as much as an analgesia one.

What this means in practice

  • TTP blocks are technically feasible in rabbits when ultrasound guidance is available. The authors placed bilateral blocks at two intercostal levels and stayed within a stated bupivacaine total dose.
  • Treat this as one piece of a multimodal plan, not a stand-alone solution. Systemic opioids, an alpha-2 agonist, a benzodiazepine, inhalant anesthesia, and a ketamine CRI were already in place. The block added targeted thoracic wall analgesia on top of that foundation.
  • Local anesthetic toxicity risk is real in small patients. Total dose and volume matter more when the patient weighs only a kilogram or two. Calculate carefully, aspirate before injection, and know your lipid emulsion protocol before you need it.
  • A quiet intraoperative patient is not the whole story. One brief nociceptive response does not prove block failure or success by itself. Watch recovery quality, respiratory pattern, and return of normal behavior.
  • Thoracic drains need active monitoring. The second rabbit's death was tied to drain malfunction and respiratory compromise, not to the block description. Check patency, output, and breathing effort frequently in the early postoperative period.

Strengths and limitations

The report shows that ultrasound-guided TTP blocks can be performed in rabbits at clinically relevant sites for sternotomy, with a clear dose and volume description. Pairing the block with a familiar multimodal systemic plan makes the approach transferable for teams that already run ketamine CRIs.

Limitations are obvious: two rabbits, no control group, and no formal pain scoring comparison. One rabbit died from a postoperative drain problem, which limits what we can say about recovery quality after TTP blockade. Extrapolation from canine and feline TTP literature remains part of the reasoning until larger rabbit series exist.

How I would use this

For a rabbit facing sternotomy or another major thoracic approach, I would still build the plan around multimodal analgesia: appropriate premedication, inhalant or balanced injectable maintenance, a ketamine CRI when indicated, and local anesthesia wherever anatomy and skill allow. This paper supports adding a bilateral TTP block when ultrasound and dosing confidence are there.

I would verify the bupivacaine calculation twice, keep injection volumes small and distributed, and brief the recovery team specifically on drain function and respiratory monitoring for the first several hours. Families should hear that thoracic surgery carries meaningful risk beyond the incision itself.

If your practice rarely performs rabbit sternotomy, referral to a clinician comfortable with exotic companion mammal anesthesia and thoracic surgery is appropriate. Referral pathways are outlined for colleagues at ../referring-veterinarians.html.
