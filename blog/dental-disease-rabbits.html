Dental Disease in Pet Rabbits

October 2026

Dental disease is extremely common in pet rabbits and is a frequent cause of reduced appetite, drooling, weight loss, and gastrointestinal slowdown. Rabbits’ teeth grow continuously throughout life. When the teeth do not wear properly—often because of diet, genetics, or injury—spurs, overgrowth, or root problems can develop.

Owners may notice a preference for soft foods, reduced hay consumption, drooling, or wetness under the chin. Because rabbits hide pain well, these signs can be subtle at first. Regular veterinary examinations that include a careful oral assessment are one of the best ways to catch problems early.

Diagnosis often involves a thorough oral exam under sedation or anesthesia and dental radiographs. Treatment can range from coronal reduction (trimming) of overgrown teeth to extraction of severely diseased teeth. Pain management is an important part of every dental procedure.

Long-term management centers on a high-fiber diet that encourages natural chewing, along with periodic rechecks. Many rabbits with dental disease can live comfortable lives with consistent care.
