Your Rabbit's Head Is Tilted. What Now?

October 27, 2026

You walk in and your rabbit's head is cocked to one side. Maybe they are circling, leaning against the wall, or rolling when they try to move. It looks awful, and most families assume the worst.

Take a breath. Head tilt is a sign, not a diagnosis, and many rabbits with it recover well or learn to live comfortably with a little lean. What matters most right now is keeping your rabbit safe and getting them seen.

Why it happens

A rabbit keeps its balance through the vestibular system, which lives in the inner ear and connects to the brain. When something disrupts that system on one side, the world feels like it is spinning, and the head turns toward the problem.

The usual causes I look for are:

  • Ear infection. Bacteria in the middle or inner ear are one of the most common causes. Lop-eared rabbits seem to be at higher risk, probably because of the shape of their ear canals.
  • Encephalitozoon cuniculi (E. cuniculi). This is a microscopic parasite many pet rabbits carry without ever getting sick. In some rabbits it causes inflammation in the brain or kidneys.
  • Injury. A fall or a scuffle with another pet can cause a tilt.
  • Less common causes. Tumors, abscesses deep in the skull, and other neurologic disease are on the list too, especially in older rabbits.

Sometimes more than one of these is going on at once. That is part of why the workup matters.

How we sort it out

I start with a full exam, including a careful look at the ears, eyes, and nervous system, and I ask a lot of questions about how it started. A sudden tilt after a fall is a different story from one that crept in over a week alongside a smelly ear.

From there, the next steps depend on the rabbit. Bloodwork tells us how the kidneys and other organs are doing. A blood test for E. cuniculi can help, but a positive result only means your rabbit has been exposed at some point. It doesn't prove E. cuniculi is causing the tilt, since so many healthy rabbits test positive.

Skull radiographs can show changes in the ear bones. Research has shown ultrasound of the ear can be useful in awake rabbits, and CT through a referral imaging center gives the clearest picture of the middle and inner ear when we need it.

Treatment is aimed at the cause, along with medication to settle the dizziness and nausea, pain relief, and support for eating. Rabbits with a tilt often stop eating, and a rabbit that doesn't eat can quickly develop gut slowdown on top of everything else.

Keeping your rabbit safe at home

This is where you make the biggest difference.

Pad the space. Rabbits with a bad tilt may roll and hurt themselves. A small, well-padded area works well. Rolled towels, a low-sided box lined with fleece, or a playpen with thick soft bedding all help. Take out ramps, shelves, and anything they could fall from.

Bring food and water to them. Put hay, greens, and a shallow water bowl right where your rabbit can reach without walking far. Some rabbits do better leaning against something soft while they eat.

Watch the eye on the down side. It can get dry or scratched against bedding. Let your vet know if it looks red, cloudy, or keeps closing.

Keep things quiet and dim. Bright, busy rooms make the spinning feel worse.

Track food and droppings. Fewer or smaller droppings are an early warning that the gut is slowing down.

Be patient. Improvement can be slow, and some rabbits keep a mild tilt for life. In my experience, many of those rabbits adapt well. They eat, groom, and enjoy their days with their head a few degrees off center.

When to call your vet

Call right away if your rabbit:

  • Develops a head tilt suddenly
  • Is rolling or can't stay upright
  • Stops eating or stops passing droppings
  • Has a seizure, seems weak, or is breathing hard
  • Has a swollen face or discharge from an ear

Book a visit soon if you notice:

  • A mild tilt that has been there a while
  • Scratching or shaking at one ear
  • A change in how your rabbit walks or balances
  • A rabbit that tested positive for E. cuniculi in the past and seems a little off
