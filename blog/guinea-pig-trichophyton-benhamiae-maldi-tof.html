Trichophyton benhamiae in a Guinea Pig: A Lab Result Worth Questioning

November 9, 2026

Most of us trust the species name that comes back on a fungal culture report. This week's case report is a good reminder that the name depends on the method, and that for guinea pig ringworm the name changes the conversation with the family.

The paper

Gül MB, Ötkün S, Başak M, Akgül G. Mycological and molecular confirmation of Trichophyton benhamiae infection in a guinea pig. Vet Res Commun. 2026;51(1):38. doi:10.1007/s11259-026-11617-2

The case

A pet guinea pig had a dermatophyte isolated from a skin scraping. On MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry, the isolate was identified as "Microsporum audouinii_canis," with a score of 2.093. That is a score most labs would accept.

The authors didn't stop there. ITS sequencing of the same isolate gave 99.64% identity to Trichophyton benhamiae.

The lesion resolved after about three weeks of topical ketoconazole cream. At the four-month follow-up, KOH preparation and culture were both negative.

Why the species matters

T. benhamiae is a zoonotic dermatophyte strongly associated with guinea pigs, and it is a recognized cause of ringworm in people who handle them, particularly children. A report that says Microsporum leads you toward a different set of assumptions about the source, and possibly toward a Wood's lamp. Trichophyton species don't fluoresce, so a negative Wood's lamp exam tells you very little in a guinea pig.

The bigger point is that MALDI-TOF libraries are only as good as their reference spectra, and dermatophytes from exotic species are not always well represented. When the identification will change what you tell the family, ask the lab whether sequencing is available.

Strengths and limitations

The strength here is the confirmation. The authors used culture, MALDI-TOF, and ITS sequencing on one isolate, and they followed the animal long enough to document a negative culture months later.

It is still a single animal with a focal lesion. One guinea pig clearing with a topical azole doesn't tell us how a guinea pig with widespread lesions, or a household with several affected animals, will respond.

How I approach guinea pig ringworm

I lean on the World Association for Veterinary Dermatology consensus statement for dogs and cats (Moriello et al., 2017), extrapolated thoughtfully, because there isn't a guinea pig equivalent.

  • Culture. My preference is a DTM culture by the toothbrush method: a sterile toothbrush brushed over the coat and skin for at least 30 seconds and submitted to the lab. Talk with your microbiology lab about how they want it.
  • PCR, with caution. It is faster, but a positive result only shows fungal DNA, not active infection. PCR-positive, culture-negative cases can be frustrating for everyone.
  • Treatment. A small focal lesion may respond to topical therapy alone, as it did here. For more extensive disease, I prefer terbinafine as a systemic drug. Comparative work in guinea pigs and hedgehogs favored terbinafine over itraconazole, and it has the lowest MIC against both Microsporum and Trichophyton in multiple studies.
  • The environment. Cleaning the cage, hides, and fleece is part of treatment. Free handouts from sources such as Veterinary Partner help families do it well.
  • Rechecks. I recheck a culture about a month into treatment and look for two negative cultures at least two weeks apart before calling it cured.
  • The people. Anyone in the home with a ring-shaped, itchy, or scaly rash should see their own physician. Hand washing after handling, and keeping young children from snuggling an affected guinea pig against their face, are reasonable advice.

I also ask where the guinea pig came from, how recently it joined the household, and whether anyone else in the home, furry or not, has skin lesions.

Questions for rounds

  1. Does your reference lab use MALDI-TOF for dermatophytes, and does it offer sequencing?
  2. At what point would you move a guinea pig from topical to systemic antifungal therapy?
  3. How do you word zoonotic risk so families take it seriously without panicking?
