Why Your Rabbit, Guinea Pig or Ferret Needs an Annual Wellness Exam

October 2026

It's easy to think a small pet that's eating and acting normally doesn't need to see the vet. But exotic companion mammals are experts at hiding illness. A yearly wellness exam is often the only chance to catch problems like dental disease, weight changes or early tumors before they become emergencies.

Small pets hide big problems

Rabbits, guinea pigs, chinchillas and many other small mammals are prey species. Instinct tells them to look healthy even when they don't feel well. Ferrets and hedgehogs can be similarly subtle. By the time an owner notices something wrong, the underlying problem has often been developing for weeks or months.

Regular exams give your veterinarian a baseline: what your pet weighs, how their teeth look, and how their heart and lungs sound when they're healthy. Small changes from one year to the next can be the earliest clue.

What happens during a wellness exam

Every species is a little different, but a thorough exotic wellness visit usually includes:

  • Weight and body condition. Even small weight changes can be significant in a tiny animal.
  • A careful dental exam. Rabbits, guinea pigs, chinchillas and degus have teeth that grow throughout life, so overgrowth and sharp points are common. Front teeth are easy to see. Back teeth need special tools, and sometimes imaging.
  • Heart, lungs and abdomen. Listening and feeling for anything unusual.
  • Skin, coat and feet. Checking for mites, fur loss, sores on the hocks or feet, and lumps.
  • Eyes, ears and nose. Looking for discharge or signs of infection.
  • Diet and housing review. Most problems in these species connect back to diet, environment or stress, so this conversation is one of the most valuable parts of the visit.

Depending on age and species, your vet may also recommend a fecal test, bloodwork or urine testing. Priest Lake Veterinary Hospital asks owners to bring a fresh stool sample to every wellness check.

Vaccines and prevention

Some exotic pets need vaccines. Rabbits can be vaccinated against rabbit hemorrhagic disease virus type 2 (RHDV2), and ferrets need rabies and distemper vaccines. A wellness visit is a good time to keep these on schedule and to talk about parasite prevention and spay or neuter options.

Senior pets benefit even more

As exotic pets age, problems like arthritis, dental disease, kidney changes, tumors and hormone-related diseases become more common. Your veterinarian may suggest more frequent checkups or routine bloodwork for older pets.

How to prepare for the visit

  • Bring your pet in a secure carrier with a towel and some familiar hay or food.
  • Bring a fresh stool sample.
  • Write down what your pet eats each day, including treats.
  • Note any changes in appetite, droppings, behavior or weight.
  • Photos of the cage or enclosure can be very helpful.

When to call your vet

A wellness visit isn't the place to wait with an urgent problem. Call right away if your pet:

  • Stops eating or eats much less
  • Produces fewer or no droppings, or has diarrhea
  • Is drooling or has a wet chin
  • Is breathing hard, sneezing heavily or has nasal discharge
  • Develops a head tilt, weakness or seizures
  • Has a lump that is growing quickly
