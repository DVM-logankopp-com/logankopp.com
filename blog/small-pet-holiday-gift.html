Before You Wrap Up a Rabbit: Small Pets as Holiday Gifts

November 2, 2026

Every year around this time, families start asking me whether a rabbit, guinea pig, or hamster would make a good gift. It's a kind thought. Small pets can be wonderful companions, and for a lot of kids they are the first animal that is really theirs.

They also come with more needs than most people expect. A few weeks of planning makes a big difference for the animal and for your family.

"Aren't small pets easier than a dog?"

Not usually. They are different, and some of them are a bigger commitment than people realize.

Rabbits can live many years with good care. They need daily exercise outside an enclosure, a diet built mostly on hay, and a vet who knows rabbits. Most are happiest living indoors as part of the family. In Tennessee, I also recommend the RHDV2 vaccine for pet rabbits, and spaying or neutering is part of good long-term health for most rabbits.

Guinea pigs are social and should live with at least one other guinea pig. They need vitamin C every day because, like people, they can't make their own.

Ferrets are playful and smart, and they need a ferret-proofed home, vaccines, and regular checkups as they get older.

Hamsters are usually solitary and active at night. A hamster woken up during the day to be held by an excited child may bite.

Rats are social, clever, and often very good with older kids. They do best in pairs or groups, and their lives are shorter than many families expect, which is worth talking through with children.

Chinchillas are active at night, need dust baths, and are sensitive to heat.

I also see hedgehogs, sugar gliders, degus, and many other companion mammals. If you're considering a species that isn't listed here and aren't sure whether we can see it, please call.

Small pets and young children

Rabbits and guinea pigs are prey animals. Most don't enjoy being picked up and carried around, and a rabbit that kicks free and falls can break its back. That is hard for a young child to understand, and it isn't their fault.

If the pet is for a child, plan on an adult being the main caregiver. Kids can help with feeding, fresh water, and quiet time on the floor, which is the safest way for a small pet and a child to spend time together.

Why January might be the better time

The holidays are loud and busy. A new rabbit or guinea pig coming home in the middle of guests, wrapping paper, and travel gets a stressful start, and stress can make a small animal sick.

Some families give the supplies or a book about the species as the gift, then bring the animal home in January when the house is quiet. A handwritten note that says "we're getting a guinea pig, and you get to help pick" can be just as exciting for a child on the morning itself.

Local rescues often have rabbits, guinea pigs, and other small pets looking for homes, many of them adults with known personalities. That can be a good fit for a family that wants to know what they're getting.

Getting ready before your pet comes home

  • Set up the enclosure first, in a quiet room away from drafts and heaters.
  • Stock up on fresh hay for rabbits, guinea pigs, and chinchillas.
  • Ask what the animal has been eating, and keep it the same for the first couple of weeks.
  • Find an exotic vet before you need one, and schedule a new pet exam within the first few weeks.

What happens at the first visit

At a new pet visit, I check weight, teeth, eyes, ears, skin, heart, and lungs, and look for parasites or signs of illness that came home with the animal. It's also when we talk through diet, housing, vaccines, and whether spaying or neutering makes sense. Please bring a fresh stool sample if you can. You leave with a plan and know who to call if something seems off.

When to call your vet

New pets are under a lot of stress, so problems can show up in the first days and weeks.

Call right away if your new pet:

  • Isn't eating, or isn't passing droppings
  • Has diarrhea, especially a young rabbit
  • Is breathing hard or seems weak or very quiet
  • Was dropped or fell, and is limping or not moving normally

Book a visit soon if you notice:

  • Sneezing, eye discharge, or crusty eyes or nose
  • Scratching, hair loss, or flaky skin
  • Overgrown nails or teeth that don't look even
