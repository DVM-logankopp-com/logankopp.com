Hedgehog Care Basics and Wobbly Hedgehog Syndrome

October 2026

African pygmy hedgehogs are curious, quirky little pets. They also have some very specific needs, and a few health problems every owner should know about. Here's a friendly guide to keeping your hedgehog healthy, including what to do if you notice wobbling or weakness.

Housing

  • Space. Hedgehogs are active at night and travel a lot, so give them as large an enclosure as you can, with a secure lid. They are excellent escape artists.
  • A solid exercise wheel. Wire or rung wheels can injure feet and legs.
  • Hiding spots. A hut, fleece pouch or tube gives them a safe place to sleep.
  • Bedding. Use soft, dust-free paper bedding or fleece. Avoid cedar, pine, corncob and scented or dusty products, which can irritate the airways.
  • Live alone. Hedgehogs are solitary and should be housed on their own.

Temperature

Hedgehogs need warmth. If they get too cold (or too hot), they can slip into a sluggish, hibernation-like state called torpor, which is dangerous for pet hedgehogs. Keep their enclosure warm and steady, generally between about 75°F and 85°F, using a safe heat source and a thermometer. A cold, limp or unresponsive hedgehog needs gentle warming and a call to your vet right away.

Diet

  • A commercial hedgehog diet or a high-quality food recommended by your vet as the main diet
  • Insects in moderation as treats, such as mealworms or crickets
  • Small amounts of fruit only occasionally
  • Measured portions. Hedgehogs gain weight easily, and obesity is a common problem that can lead to fatty liver, heart strain and difficulty curling up.
  • Fresh water every day
  • Avoid raw meat or eggs, milk, nuts, seeds and hard human foods

Daily handling and hygiene

Hedgehogs can carry Salmonella bacteria without looking sick. Wash your hands after handling your hedgehog or cleaning the enclosure, avoid kissing or snuggling them near your face, and keep them out of kitchens and food areas. Young children, older adults and people with weakened immune systems should take extra care.

What is wobbly hedgehog syndrome?

Wobbly hedgehog syndrome (WHS) is a progressive neurological disease that affects pet hedgehogs. It damages the protective coating around nerves in the brain and spinal cord. The exact cause isn't fully understood, though a genetic component is suspected.

Signs of WHS

  • Wobbling or losing balance, often first in the back legs
  • Falling over or trouble staying upright
  • Tripping or dragging the feet
  • Difficulty curling into a ball
  • Weight loss and muscle wasting as the disease progresses
  • Eventually, weakness spreading to the front legs and difficulty eating

WHS is a gradual, progressive disease. Sadly, there is no cure, and a definite diagnosis can only be made after death. Your veterinarian can make a presumptive diagnosis based on signs and by ruling out other conditions.

Rule out other causes first

Wobbling isn't always WHS. Spinal injury, tumors, infections, stroke, nutritional problems, being too cold, and other illnesses can look similar, and some of these are treatable. That's why an exam, and sometimes bloodwork, X-rays or advanced imaging, is so important.

Supportive care

Many hedgehogs with WHS can enjoy good quality time with supportive care:

  • Soft, flat flooring and low food and water dishes
  • Hand-feeding or soft foods as needed
  • Keeping them clean and warm
  • Gentle physical therapy, as advised by your vet
  • Regular rechecks to keep them comfortable

Your vet will help you watch quality of life and make kind decisions when the time comes.

When to call your vet

Call your veterinarian if your hedgehog:

  • Is wobbling, falling over or dragging their legs
  • Is eating less or losing weight
  • Is cold, limp or unusually sluggish
  • Has quill loss, flaky skin or scratches a lot
  • Has a lump, bad breath or trouble eating
