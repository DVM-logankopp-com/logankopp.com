Why Is My Rabbit Sneezing? Snuffles and Other Causes

November 18, 2026

Rabbits sneeze. A single sneeze after dusty hay, followed by a return to eating, is common.

The sneeze that should get your attention is the one that keeps happening, or comes with a wet nose, sticky eyes, or crusty fur on the inside of the front legs. Rabbits groom their faces with their front paws, so matted, damp fur there is often the first clue that something is running from the nose.

What "snuffles" means

Snuffles is an old nickname for upper respiratory infection in rabbits: sneezing, nasal discharge, and sometimes goopy eyes. For years it was blamed almost entirely on one bacterium, Pasteurella multocida.

Pasteurella is real and it does cause disease. But it's only part of the picture. Plenty of rabbits carry Pasteurella in their nose and never get sick from it. A recent study found it in a few rabbits that looked completely healthy, as well as in rabbits with dental abscesses. Other bacteria can cause the same signs. So "snuffles" describes what the rabbit is doing, not a diagnosis.

Why the teeth matter

Rabbit teeth grow throughout life, and the roots of the upper cheek teeth and incisors sit very close to the nasal passages and tear ducts. When those roots overgrow or become infected, they can press on or drain into the nose. The result looks exactly like a respiratory infection.

That's why a rabbit with a runny nose needs a careful mouth exam, and often skull X-rays or a CT scan. Antibiotics alone won't fix a tooth problem, and a rabbit treated for "snuffles" over and over may actually have dental disease underneath. You can read more in my post on dental disease in rabbits.

Other things that cause sneezing

  • Dust and irritants. Dusty hay, scented litters, air fresheners, and smoke all irritate a rabbit's nose.
  • Ammonia. Urine-soaked litter that isn't changed often enough gives off ammonia, which is hard on the airways.
  • Something stuck in the nose. A grass seed or bit of hay can cause sudden, repeated sneezing, sometimes on one side.
  • Tear duct problems. Blocked or infected tear ducts cause watery or white, milky eyes and can go hand in hand with nasal signs.

How we figure out the cause

Your rabbit's history and exam come first, including the teeth. From there I may recommend imaging of the skull, and a culture of the nasal discharge or of any abscess. A culture tells us which bacteria are present and helps choose a medication. It's a lot better than guessing, especially for a rabbit who has already had several rounds of antibiotics.

Please don't give your rabbit human cold medicine, decongestants, or leftover antibiotics. Some of these are dangerous for rabbits, and the wrong antibiotic can seriously upset a rabbit's gut.

Things you can do at home

  • Choose a low-dust hay and shake it out away from your rabbit's space
  • Use unscented paper or similar low-dust litter, and change it often
  • Skip air fresheners, candles, and smoking near your rabbit
  • Gently wipe crusts from the nose and eyes with a soft damp cloth if your rabbit tolerates it
  • Keep an eye on appetite and droppings, because a rabbit who can't smell food well may eat less

Middle Tennessee winters can be a mix of dry indoor heat and closed-up houses. That combination doesn't cause infection on its own, but it can make a sensitive nose worse, so good ventilation and a clean litter box help.

When to call your vet

Call right away if your rabbit:

  • Is breathing with the mouth open, or breathing loudly or with effort. Rabbits normally breathe through the nose, so open-mouth breathing is an emergency.
  • Has a blue or gray tinge to the lips or tongue
  • Stops eating or stops producing droppings
  • Has a head tilt, loss of balance, or a swelling on the face or jaw

Book a visit soon if you notice:

  • Sneezing that keeps happening over more than a day or two
  • White, yellow, or green discharge from the nose or eyes
  • Crusty or matted fur on the inside of the front legs
  • Watery eyes or a wet face
  • Sneezing that keeps coming back after treatment
