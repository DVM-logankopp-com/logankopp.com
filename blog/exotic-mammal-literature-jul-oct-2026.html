Exotic Companion Mammal Literature Roundup: July to October 2026

October 2026

Keeping up with the rabbit, ferret and guinea pig literature is hard when you're also running appointments. This quarter's roundup from Dr. Logan Kopp, Nashville's exotic companion mammal specialist, covers seven papers that touch on things we encounter in practice all the time. Each summary is based on the published abstract, so treat these as starting points to look into rather than settled protocols.

Key findings

Rabbits

1. Towel-wrap ("bunny burrito") restraint for conscious CT. In 93 client-owned rabbits, the burrito (n = 36) produced fewer pre-contrast motion artifacts (p = 0.02), better positioning in dental cases (p = 0.03) and better early post-contrast stability (p = 0.04) than a plexiglass chamber (n = 47). Image quality was descriptively similar to sedation (n = 10), with no complications. Option to consider: for head and dental CT, a well-done burrito may let some rabbits skip sedation.

2. Traumatic jaw fractures on CT. Of 21 rabbits, 85.7% had mandibular ramus fractures (14 unilateral, 4 bilateral), and concurrent fractures were common. Ten injuries were accidental owner-inflicted trauma. Of 13 rabbits with follow-up, 8 developed incisor malocclusion and 6 cheek-tooth malocclusion. Eleven were alive 581 days after trauma. Option to consider: CT for head trauma, plus a long-term dental recheck plan.

3. Bulla ultrasound for otitis media, unsedated. With CT as the reference in 15 rabbits (30 ears), ultrasound could evaluate 29 of 30 bullae in a mean of 9.8 minutes per rabbit, with 88.3% sensitivity and 79.2% specificity (κ = 0.496). Option to consider: a sedation-free first screen, with CT kept for equivocal cases and surgical planning.

4. Ahmed valve gonioimplants for refractory glaucoma. In 6 rabbits (8 eyes), Holland Lops were overrepresented. Median IOP fell from 32 to 17 mmHg and vision was retained in all eyes for a mean of 11.6 months. Mean antiglaucoma drugs fell only from 1.75 to 1.38. Option to consider: an ophthalmology referral conversation for lops with refractory glaucoma.

Ferrets

5. Urethral obstruction: better outcomes than euthanasia rates suggest. In 68 ferrets, causes were urolithiasis (57.4%), adrenal-associated prostatic disease (19.1%) and undiagnosed (23.5%). 40.9% were euthanized at presentation, yet 92.3% (36/39) of treated ferrets survived to discharge. Complications occurred in 23.1% and recurrence in 31.6%. Option to consider: these numbers can help when counseling owners before they decide.

6. Glucometers and hypoglycemia. In 24 ferrets, classification agreement with a chemistry analyzer at ≤60 mg/dL was 79% (AlphaTrak3 canine/feline settings), 75% (equine) and 58% (human meter). I'm a coauthor. Full summary here. Option to consider: confirm low or borderline meter readings before acting on them.

Guinea pigs

7. Bacterial isolates and urine culture. Of 112 isolates from 63 ill guinea pigs, Streptococcus (19%), Corynebacterium (17%) and Prevotella (11%) led. Only 50% of urinary isolates were TMS-susceptible and 59% enrofloxacin-susceptible. Full summary here. Option to consider: culture before relying on empiric choices.

What this means in practice

None of these papers is a reason to overhaul a protocol overnight. A few themes stand out: lower-stress imaging options for rabbits, caution with point-of-care numbers, and culturing before relying on habit. They're worth keeping in mind the next time one of these cases walks in.
