Hedgehog veterinary care in Nashville

Hedgehogs hide illness under the quills. Weight loss, a change in the way they walk, or a smell from the mouth is often the first thing a family notices. Dr. Kopp is an exotic companion mammal specialist. He sees African pygmy hedgehogs and other pet hedgehogs at Priest Lake Veterinary Hospital in Antioch, for primary care and referral cases.

Many hedgehog visits need a sedated exam, because a rolled-up hedgehog cannot be examined fully while awake. We talk through that, and through any treatment that rests on limited evidence. More on home care is in wobbly hedgehog syndrome and hedgehog care.

Wobbly hedgehog syndrome and neurologic disease

Wobbling, weakness, falling, or trouble using the hind legs needs a call. Wobbly hedgehog syndrome is one cause. It is a progressive neurologic disease, and the visit is an evaluation. Other causes are considered at the same time, including ear disease, trauma, nutrition problems, and masses. Dr. Kopp is trained in treating these neurologic problems. Supportive care, pain control, and a plan for eating and moving are built around what the hedgehog can still do.

  • Wobbly hedgehog syndrome evaluation
  • Ataxia, paresis, and other neurologic disorders
  • Sedated exams and imaging when indicated
  • Supportive care and palliative care

Hedgehog oral cancer and cancer care

Oral squamous cell carcinoma is a common tumor in hedgehogs. Drooling, a swollen jaw, blood from the mouth, or weight loss needs a call while the hospital is open. Always call first at (615) 361-4646. Skin tumors, mammary tumors, uterine tumors, and lymphoma are also seen. Dr. Kopp is trained in treating these cancers. When cancer is diagnosed, we discuss all options, including surgery, chemotherapy, and comfort care, and build a plan around your pet and your family's goals. Radiation therapy is arranged through a referral facility when it is indicated.

  • Oral squamous cell carcinoma and other oral tumors
  • Cutaneous, mammary, and uterine tumors
  • Lymphoma
  • Cytology, biopsy, and imaging for staging when indicated
  • Tumor removal and ovariohysterectomy when indicated
  • Comfort care
  • Palliative care and cancer pain control

Hedgehog dental disease and oral infection

Periodontal disease and oral infection can look like a tumor until the mouth is examined under sedation. Call if your hedgehog is dropping food, losing weight, or has a foul smell from the mouth. Skull and dental radiographs are taken in the hospital for dental evaluation.

  • Dental and periodontal disease
  • Oral infections
  • Sedated oral exams
  • Skull and dental radiographs taken in the hospital for dental evaluation

Hedgehog skin and quills

Quill loss, itch, crusts, or hairless patches of skin can be mites, ringworm, or another infection. Ringworm can pass to people. Bring a note about new bedding and whether anyone in the home has a rash.

  • Mites and dermatophytosis
  • Quill loss and skin infections
  • Skin scrapings and fungal testing when indicated

Hedgehog reproductive disease and surgery

Uterine disease is a reason some hedgehogs are spayed. Ovariohysterectomy and other soft-tissue surgery, including tumor removal, are performed when they are the right treatment. Anesthesia is planned for a patient that may already be losing weight.

  • Reproductive and uterine disease
  • Ovariohysterectomy
  • Tumor removal and other soft-tissue surgery

Hedgehog GI, liver, and respiratory disease

A hedgehog that stops eating can have dental disease, cancer, liver disease, or a gut problem. Green or soft stool, a big belly, or labored breathing needs a call. Obesity and diet are reviewed, because extra weight makes anesthesia and mobility harder. Pneumonia and other respiratory infections are treated as urgent when the hedgehog is working to breathe.

  • Gastrointestinal disease and hepatic disease
  • Obesity and nutrition counseling
  • Respiratory infection and pneumonia
  • Bloodwork and imaging when indicated

Hedgehog anesthesia and emergency care

Sedated exams and imaging are a routine part of hedgehog medicine. The hospital provides high-quality anesthesia, with dedicated anesthesia technicians and full monitoring (ECG, SpO2, capnography, blood pressure, and temperature). Dr. Kopp is a Fellow in Companion Animal Pain Management (FABVP-CAPM). That fellowship is separate from his board certification in Exotic Companion Mammal Practice. Pain control and palliative care matter for cancer and for wobbly hedgehog syndrome. A collapsed, cold, or open-mouth breathing hedgehog needs a call before you drive. During hospital hours, Priest Lake Veterinary Hospital may be able to see emergency cases. Always call first at (615) 361-4646.

  • Sedated exams and imaging
  • Anesthetic planning and monitoring
  • Pain management, palliative care, and geriatric care
  • Emergency care if we can take the case

What a visit is like

Bring the hedgehog in a secure carrier with a hide. A recent weight and a short video of the walk, if your hedgehog is wobbling, help the exam. Many procedures wait until the hedgehog can be sedated safely, so the first visit may be an assessment and a plan for that sedation.

When to call

Call while the hospital is open if your hedgehog:

  • Is wobbling, falling, or weaker in the hind legs
  • Is losing weight, drooling, or bleeding from the mouth
  • Has a new lump, quill loss, or crusted skin
  • Is breathing hard, collapsed, or cold
  • Has stopped eating
