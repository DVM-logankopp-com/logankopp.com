Referring an exotic companion mammal

For primary care veterinarians in Nashville and Middle Tennessee.

Dr. Logan Kopp, DVM, DABVP (Exotic Companion Mammal Practice), FABVP-CAPM, is Nashville's exotic companion mammal specialist. He is the only veterinarian currently practicing in Tennessee as a Diplomate in Exotic Companion Mammal Practice, and he sees referral cases at Priest Lake Veterinary Hospital. That includes rabbits, ferrets, guinea pigs, chinchillas, hedgehogs, sugar gliders, rats, mice, degus, and other legally owned companion mammals such as foxes and squirrels. He does not treat birds or reptiles.

Priest Lake is a general hospital that includes this Exotic Companion Mammal Practice. It should not be described as a specialty hospital, because not every veterinarian on staff holds ABVP certification. Patients go back to the referring clinic for ongoing care.

How to refer

Send the hospital referral form, call (615) 361-4646, or email info@priestlakevet.com. The hospital is at 2445 Morris Gentry Blvd, Nashville, TN 37013. If the animal is painful, bloated, or shocky, call before transfer instead of finishing the workup.

After hours, Dr. Kopp consults with Veterinary Emergency Group in Franklin, Murfreesboro, and West Nashville and can provide surgical care for exotic companion mammal emergencies when that team asks.

What to send

A short history is more useful than a full chart dump. Please include:

  • History: timeline, diet, last meal, fecal output, and current weight
  • Medications already given, with doses and times
  • Prior diagnostics and imaging, including radiographs or a CT if you already have them
  • Laboratory results

Common reasons to refer

  • Dental disease. Dental cases have their own checklist.
  • Gastrointestinal stasis, especially a rabbit that has stopped eating. GI stasis referrals have their own note.
  • Surgery, including spay and neuter and mass removal
  • Ferret adrenal disease and insulinoma
  • Urinary disease
  • Imaging, including CT
  • Anesthesia for a high-risk patient
  • Pain that is hard to recognize or hard to control
