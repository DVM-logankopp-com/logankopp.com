Rat and mouse veterinary care in Nashville

Rats and mice are small, and they decline quickly once they stop eating or start working to breathe. Dr. Kopp is an exotic companion mammal specialist. He sees pet, fancy, and hairless rats, and pet and fancy mice, at Priest Lake Veterinary Hospital in Antioch. Visits include primary care and cases sent by other veterinarians.

Hamsters and gerbils are welcome too. See hamster, gerbil, and degu care. We will tell you what we know, and what is still uncertain, before we treat. A home guide is rat, mouse, and hamster health problems.

Rat respiratory disease treatment

Sneezing, noisy breathing, porphyrin staining around the nose, or a rat that breathes harder after exercise is a reason to call. Chronic respiratory disease in rats is often tied to Mycoplasma, and it can flare into pneumonia. Open-mouth breathing, blue or pale gums, or a rat that is too tired to eat needs a call before you drive. Always call first at (615) 361-4646. Mice get respiratory infections and chronic respiratory disease as well. Care can include oxygen and other respiratory support.

  • Chronic respiratory disease in rats, including Mycoplasma-associated disease
  • Pneumonia and respiratory distress
  • Respiratory infections and chronic respiratory disease in mice
  • Respiratory supportive care when indicated

Rat mammary tumors and cancer care

A lump on a rat is often a mammary tumor. It may sit far from a nipple. Mammary fibroadenomas and mammary carcinomas are both seen. Many are removed surgically, and the surgery is easier while the lump is small. Pituitary tumors can show up as a head tilt, weakness, or trouble holding food. Mice are seen for mammary tumors and other masses. Dr. Kopp is trained in treating these tumors. When cancer is diagnosed, we discuss all options, including surgery, chemotherapy, and comfort care, and build a plan around your pet and your family's goals. Radiation therapy is arranged through a referral facility when it is indicated. Read rat mammary tumor care.

  • Mammary fibroadenoma and mammary carcinoma in rats
  • Pituitary tumors and other cancer in rats
  • Mammary tumors and other masses in mice
  • Cytology or biopsy when it changes the plan
  • Surgical mass removal, including rat mammary tumor removal
  • Comfort care and cancer pain control

Rat and mouse neurologic disease

A head tilt, a seizure, or hind limb weakness in a rat can be a pituitary tumor, an inner ear problem, trauma, or degenerative disease. Call if the rat cannot eat, falls, or seems painful. Obesity and osteoarthritis are common in older rats and are part of geriatric visits. Mice are seen for neurologic disease as well. Pain control is planned for what the animal can still do at home.

  • Head tilt, seizures, and other neurologic disease
  • Hind limb weakness and pituitary tumors in rats
  • Degenerative musculoskeletal disease and osteoarthritis
  • Neurologic disease in mice
  • Obesity

Rat and mouse skin, dental, and reproductive care

Abscesses, mites, lice, barbering, and other skin disease are common. In mice, ulcerative dermatitis and fight wounds are common too. Overgrown incisors need trimming or another dental plan under anesthesia when the teeth are diseased. Skull and dental radiographs are taken in the hospital for dental evaluation. Gastrointestinal and urinary problems are worked up when the signs fit. Spay and neuter are available. An early spay is one option families discuss when they want to lower the chance of mammary tumors in rats. Lumps can still appear later, so keep checking.

  • Skin abscesses, mites, lice, barbering, and other skin disease
  • Ulcerative dermatitis, ectoparasites, and fight wounds in mice
  • Pododermatitis
  • Dental disease and malocclusion
  • Gastrointestinal disease and urinary disease
  • Reproductive disease, spay, and neuter
  • Nutrition counseling

Rat surgery, anesthesia, and pain management

Rats are commonly seen for mammary tumor removal. Selected mass removal and reproductive surgery are also performed in mice, with anesthesia planned for a very small patient. The hospital provides high-quality anesthesia, with dedicated anesthesia technicians and full monitoring (ECG, SpO2, capnography, blood pressure, and temperature). Dr. Kopp is a Fellow in Companion Animal Pain Management (FABVP-CAPM). That fellowship is separate from his board certification in Exotic Companion Mammal Practice. Chronic pain and palliative care are part of geriatric medicine. Some fractures can be repaired in partnership with board-certified veterinary surgeons (DACVS). Others are referred. See chronic pain in older small pets and preparing for surgery.

  • Surgical mass removal and mammary tumor surgery
  • Spay, neuter, and other reproductive surgery
  • Selected mass removal in mice
  • Anesthesia for rats and for small mouse patients
  • Pain control, chronic pain management, and palliative care
  • Geriatric medicine

What a visit is like

Bring the rat or mouse in a secure carrier with a familiar hide. A recent weight, a photo of the lump if there is one, and a note about breathing at home are useful. Handling stays quiet. A patient that is working to breathe is seen as soon as the day allows.

When to call

Call while the hospital is open if your rat or mouse:

  • Is breathing harder, sneezing, or breathing with the mouth open
  • Has a new lump, or a lump that is growing
  • Has a head tilt, seizures, or hind limb weakness
  • Has stopped eating, or is bleeding or badly injured
  • Has sores, fight wounds, or incisors that are overgrown

The hospital is open Monday through Friday, 7:30 AM to 7:00 PM. Saturday and Sunday are closed.
