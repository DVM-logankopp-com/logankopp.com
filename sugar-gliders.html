Sugar glider veterinary care in Nashville

Sugar gliders are small, nocturnal, and social. Many of the illnesses that bring them in start with diet or with housing. Dr. Kopp is an exotic companion mammal specialist. He sees sugar gliders for primary care and referral cases at Priest Lake Veterinary Hospital in Antioch.

Bring the full diet, including any homemade mix, and tell us who the glider lives with. Sugar glider medicine has real gaps, so nothing starts until we have talked it through. Home care is in sugar glider nutrition and metabolic bone disease.

Sugar glider metabolic bone disease and nutrition

Hind limb weakness, tremors, or a fracture after ordinary activity can be metabolic bone disease. The usual background is a diet too low in calcium, too high in phosphorus, or short on other nutrients. Obesity is a separate nutrition problem and is reviewed at the same visit. Call if your glider is weak, shaking, or reluctant to climb. Bring what you are already feeding, and wait to change the diet until the visit.

  • Nutritional secondary hyperparathyroidism
  • Metabolic bone disease and calcium-phosphorus imbalance
  • Hind limb weakness from nutritional disease
  • Other nutritional deficiencies
  • Obesity
  • Diet counseling

Sugar glider dental disease

Facial swelling, dropping food, or a glider that cries when chewing can be a dental abscess or periodontal disease. Call while the hospital is open if the face is swollen or the glider stops eating. Always call first at (615) 361-4646. Skull and dental radiographs are taken in the hospital for dental evaluation. Treatment is planned around anesthesia and pain control for a very small patient.

  • Dental and periodontal disease
  • Dental abscesses and facial swelling
  • Skull and dental radiographs taken in the hospital for dental evaluation
  • Dental treatment under anesthesia when indicated

Sugar glider self-mutilation and behavior

A glider chewing a limb, the tail, or a surgical site needs a call while the hospital is open. Always call first at (615) 361-4646. Pain, infection, stress, and social housing problems are all part of the workup. A glider housed alone, or a group that is fighting, should be described when you call. Wound care starts at the visit. The housing plan is part of treatment.

  • Self-mutilation
  • Behavioral stress
  • Social housing problems
  • Wound care for chew injuries

Sugar glider skin and GI disease

Hair loss, scabs, and parasites are evaluated with the housing and the diet. Diarrhea and dehydration move quickly in a glider this size. A glider that is limp, cold, or not eating needs a call before you drive, while the hospital is open.

  • Skin disease and skin infections
  • Parasites
  • Gastrointestinal disease and diarrhea
  • Dehydration and fluid support

Sugar glider urinary and reproductive disease

Urinary disease, reproductive disease, pouch infections, and mastitis are seen in sugar gliders. A joey in trouble, a swollen pouch, or a female that is straining needs a call while the hospital is open. Always call first at (615) 361-4646.

  • Urinary disease
  • Reproductive disease and reproductive emergencies
  • Pouch infections and mastitis
  • Castration for selected gliders

Sugar glider trauma, fractures, and anesthesia

Falls, cage injuries, and fractures are common reasons for an urgent visit. Some fractures can be repaired in partnership with board-certified veterinary surgeons (DACVS). Others are referred. Soft-tissue surgery, castration, and wound closure are performed when they fit the patient. Dr. Kopp is a Fellow in Companion Animal Pain Management (FABVP-CAPM). That fellowship is separate from his board certification in Exotic Companion Mammal Practice. The hospital provides high-quality anesthesia, with dedicated anesthesia technicians and full monitoring (ECG, SpO2, capnography, blood pressure, and temperature), planned for a small marsupial, with warmth and pain control.

  • Trauma and fractures
  • Some fractures repaired in partnership with board-certified veterinary surgeons (DACVS), and others referred
  • Soft-tissue surgery and castration
  • Wound management
  • High-quality anesthesia with dedicated anesthesia technicians and full monitoring
  • Pain control

What a visit is like

Use a secure carrier with a hide. Check that the carrier latch is closed. A written diet and a photo of the cage help more than a general description. If the glider is chewing a limb on the way in, call ahead so the team knows you are coming.

When to call

Call while the hospital is open if your sugar glider:

  • Is weak in the hind legs, shaking, or unable to climb
  • Has a swollen face, or has stopped eating
  • Is chewing a limb, the tail, or a wound
  • Has diarrhea, is cold, or is limp
  • Has a pouch problem, mastitis, or trouble giving birth
  • Fell, is bleeding, or may have a fracture
